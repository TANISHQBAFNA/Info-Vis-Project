    01 · Establishing

    Virginia is not one housing market.

    Color is a typical home, live from Zillow, for every county and independent city. The spread is the story. Scroll — the camera goes in.

    02 · Falls Church

    The ceiling is a city you can miss on the map.

    Falls Church. Independent city, Northern Virginia. A typical home here is the top of this commonwealth — over a million dollars — packed into a shape smaller than a county label.

    03 · Buchanan

    Drive west. Same state. One-fifteenth the house.

    Buchanan County, coalfields. The cheapest typical home on this map. Nothing about the color scale was decoration: it was this distance.

    04 · Pull back

    Fifteen times apart, still one commonwealth.

    The camera lets go. Two marks. Falls Church and Buchanan stay lit. Everyone else recedes. This is the range a “Virginia home” has to mean.

    05 · Fairfax

    Most of the expensive story sits here.

    Fairfax County is not the peak and not the floor. It is Northern Virginia’s typical home — live ZHVI, this month — the place the rest of the film holds on.

    06 · The ratio

    The house is years of rent, stacked.

    Same county, new color: typical home ÷ (typical rent × 12). Fairfax costs about twenty-five years of asking rent. The map is no longer dollars. It is time.

    07 · The walk

    Watch Fairfax climb the 25-year diagonal.

    The map leaves the frame. Each year is a step in rent × price space. The dashed lines are 15, 25, 35 years of rent to buy. Play the decade. The path is the argument.

    08 · The cone

    Next year is a forecast. 2031 is not.

    Year 1 of the home cone is Zillow’s metro call for Washington, DC — not Washington, Indiana. After that the last decade continues, ± a year’s volatility. A trend, held out. Not a priced model.

    09 · Cut

    Mumbai does not publish that walk.

    No 10-year ward price. No official locality rent. What exists is a stamp-duty floor for 24 BMC wards — the ready reckoner — and a census of who was counted in 2011. The camera changes cities.

    10 · M East

    Start where the floor is low and the count is high.

    M East, eastern suburbs. Ready reckoner, not a listing. A cheap stamp-duty floor on a ward that held a lot of households in 2011.

    11 · Malabar Hill

    Same instrument. The top of 24.

    Ward D. Malabar Hill. The camera crossed the harbour. The ready reckoner did not change method. The floor did.

    12 · Two homes

    Not a hectare. A thousand square feet.

    Both marks stay. Color is the ready-reckoner value of 1,000 carpet ft². That is the Mumbai version of the Virginia ratio — a home, not a hectare.

    13 · Ladder

    Now the ready reckoner, as a ranking.

    The map sits down. Twenty-four stamp-duty floors, tallest first. Malabar Hill at the top. M East in the cheap half. This is the document, not the geography.

    14 · Stock

    The people on this map are from 2011.

    Households versus the floor. Density is not where the stamp is highest. Island city in clay, suburbs in sage. Old, official, not weather.
